- **Status:** research only. Nothing has been published or bought, and none of the owner's files has been changed.

## Decision: **PILOT FIRST** (confidence: MEDIUM)

**Launch a small, gated pilot of calm Japanese flute music under the meditating masked-samurai identity**, after fixing three
things: the quotes, the provenance records, and the music's flow. Test soft flute lo-fi as the alternative.

**Why not LAUNCH:**
- 14 assets are BLOCKED. The quote set presents modern authors and misattributions as samurai wisdom, and both renders that burn
  those quotes in are blocked too.
- 37 assets NEED EVIDENCE: no generation records or plan proof for the music, the Kling clip or the images.
- There is no measured advantage for any direction (C03, C06).

**Why not DO NOT LAUNCH:** in the calm meditation lane, new channels with this kind of material demonstrably broke out (C07).
Production is cheap with tools you already have, and a capped pilot can prove the strategy wrong for about one to two months of
tool costs.

**Why not RESEARCH FURTHER:** the decision-changing unknowns — listener response to *your* music and visuals, and whether YouTube
distributes a new channel's videos — are private to a channel and can only be measured by publishing (C13).

## The three most important uncertainties
1. **Distribution.** Will YouTube show a brand-new channel's videos to anyone? In every lane, a typical new upload gets tens to
   hundreds of views, and outcomes hinge on rare breakouts (C04, C08).
2. **Listening quality.** Do listeners stay for 45 minutes? Retention is private for every competitor (C13). Your set has two
   promises mixed together, abrupt restarts and a similar-sounding group (C25–C27).
3. **Rights durability.** You hold contractual permission, not exclusive copyright (C18):
   - Kling's commercial right may end with the membership (C16).
   - ElevenLabs gives no IP warranty and says output may not be unique (C14).
   - Content ID is not available to you, while someone else's claim remains possible (C19).

## Answers to the fourteen questions

1. **Niche.** Calm Japanese flute music for meditation and quiet focus: the meditation-music lane, *not* a guided-breathing
   product.
   - **Why this lane:** it fits your music and your meditating character. New channels in it broke out (Seiiki, Ronin Soul).
     Small channels' uploads there sit near the top of the four directions (C06).
   - **What the data does not show:** an audience advantage over the alternatives. Inside channels, labels change nothing (C03).
2. **Primary listener.** Adults who keep calm music on while meditating, reading or doing deep work. Worldwide, English-speaking
   first. This is INFERRED from search and autocomplete; the pilot measures it.
3. **Need.** To quiet the mind and hold attention without being pulled away: calm without sleepiness, presence without
   distraction.
4. **Sound.**
   - Shakuhachi or bamboo flute melody over soft harp or koto and a warm low pad, free time or a slow soft pulse.
   - No drum hits.
   - Gentle starts with crossfaded joins.
   - Dynamics left more open than the current master.
   - Your six free or soft-pulse tracks are the seed. The six beat-clear tracks belong to the lo-fi arm.
5. **Length to test first.** About 45 minutes, which matches your first video and fits the generation quota. Hold it fixed across
   the pilot. Sleep-length videos (3–10 h) are postponed: they need far more unique music than the quota allows, and their
   listeners search "no ads" (C34, C35).
6. **The Black Samurai as identity.**
   - **Useful as a visual signature, unproven as a draw.** A recurring masked character on a flat backdrop already exists in samurai
     lo-fi, so it is not differentiation by itself.
   - "Black samurai" has no observed listening demand (C31).
   - **Keep the character fictional.** Don't use "Yasuke" or historical claims in titles; the search space around them is a
     game controversy.
   - Test the character in thumbnails with Test & Compare.
   - **Check the channel name before investing:** "Black Samurai" is also a 1976 film and a reggae artist. A search result is not
     trademark clearance.
7. **Visuals.**
   - **Keep:** the character, the seamless backdrop, the breathing loop, the slow-motion pauses, and a subtle speaker light if the
     viewer test doesn't flag it.
   - **Remove from the calm promise:**
     - the rolling quote scroll (a new text every 11 s competes with reading and meditation);
     - the scrambled-letter effect;
     - the fire text.
   - **At most:** one corrected, credited quote card at the opening.
   - These are INFERRED. The V1/V2/V3 test decides (C30).
8. **What makes it distinct.** Not the format: everyone in the lane uses one calm scene and long runtimes. The distinction can be:
   - one consistent character the viewer recognises across videos;
   - a recognisable flute-and-harp sound;
   - honest, credited text instead of fake "ancient quotes".

   This is UNTESTED. Distinctiveness has to show up in Test & Compare and returning viewers.
9. **Commercial use under the documented terms.**
   - **Music (ElevenLabs):** contractually yes for monetized YouTube on a paid plan — CONDITIONALLY, once the plan at generation is
     documented. Disclose AI music. No downloads or streamer libraries.
   - **Images (OpenAI):** yes — CONDITIONALLY, once provenance is recorded.
   - **Kling loop:** yes while the membership runs. The position after cancellation needs Kling's written answer.
   - **Fonts (OFL):** yes.
   - **Quotes:** not as they are.
   - **The light-and-scroll renders:** BLOCKED. The plain `final_video.mp4` NEEDS EVIDENCE.
10. **Remaining legal uncertainties.**
    - AI output has little or no copyright protection (C18), so you cannot stop copies and Content ID is unlikely.
    - Kling rights after cancellation.
    - Whether ElevenLabs output can be registered with distributors or Content ID.
    - Whether the Dominican tax treatment counts YouTube income as Dominican-source (C33).
    - The channel-name trademark position.
    - **Who to ask:** written vendor answers for Kling and ElevenLabs, a Dominican accountant for tax, and a trademark search or
      lawyer for the name.
11. **Affordable volume.**
    - **On the current tools** (ElevenLabs Creator + Kling ≈ $55/month list): about one 45-minute video a month.
    - **Music-first** (ElevenLabs Pro ≈ $99, Kling paused once its rights answer allows): about 3–5 a month.

    INFERRED, MEDIUM (C34).
12. **Smallest defensible pilot.** Five genuinely distinct ~45-minute videos: three calm-meditation and two flute lo-fi, one a
    week, with gates and preregistered rules (`pilot_plan.md`). They need about 180 minutes of new music.
13. **What justifies scaling.**
    - At least 2 independent calm uploads at or above the matched-age median of comparable small channels by day 28.
    - Browse and Suggested traffic present.
    - Retention holding past the opening.
    - No open rights blocker.
    - Your logged hours and cost per video inside the limit you set beforehand.
14. **What would change the recommendation.**
    - The lo-fi arm winning on views and watch time at matched age.
    - Blind listeners preferring the beat tracks.
    - The viewer test showing the character or the scroll distracts.
    - Vendor answers that narrow your rights.
    - Any reused or inauthentic-content action.
    - All five videos below p25 by day 56 despite real impressions — stop or pause.
